Sheridan School District · Arkansas River Co-op

Top quartile in the state. Middle of the pack against its own peers.

Sheridan's B ranks 58th of 255 Arkansas districts — genuinely good. But Sheridan also enrolls fewer low-income students than 90% of districts in the state, and the ten districts that most resemble it demographically scored a median 29 points higher. Both facts are true, and which one you lead with depends on why you're asking.

The headline

Three defensible rankings, three different stories

The A question

What actually separates Sheridan from an A district

District letter grades come from eleven indicator ratings on a 1–4 scale, not from the total score, which is why A and B point ranges overlap. Comparing Sheridan's rating profile to the median A district shows exactly where the gap is — and where it is not.

Reading the profile

  • Sheridan already rates like an A district on readiness. Graduation and merit both score 3, matching the A median and beating the B median of 2. Only 18% of A districts even score a 4 on graduation.
  • The gap is depth, not direction. A districts post a 4 on all three achievement indicators and on growth; Sheridan posts 3s. Nothing here is broken — it is the difference between solid and exceptional.
  • Two ratings of 2 do the damage. Math growth and lowest-quartile ELA and math. Those three indicators are a third of the formula, and they are the same three where Sheridan's statewide percentile falls below 45.
  • Five of Sheridan's ten demographic twins earned an A with the same kind of students, the same scale and, in several cases, higher poverty. The A is reachable; the route runs through growth.

Method

How these comparisons were built

Definitions

  • Nearby is straight-line distance between the district office coordinates published in My School Info, capped at 25 miles, limited to traditional districts. Poyen is closest at 14 miles; Malvern the furthest included at 23.5.
  • Similar size is 2024–25 enrollment within 20% of Sheridan's 4,128, which yields 15 districts from Greene County Tech (3,334) to Siloam Springs (4,546).
  • Twins are the ten nearest districts in standardized distance across low-income share, log enrollment and special education rate — a plain nearest-neighbor match, not a causal model.
  • Poverty adjustment is a least-squares line of total score on low-income share across 249 districts. A district's residual is how far above or below that line it landed.

Limits worth stating out loud

  • The poverty line is weak. r = −0.37 and residuals scatter with a standard deviation of 59.5 points, so poverty explains only about 14% of the variance between districts. Sheridan's +11.4 residual is well inside the noise — treat it as "unremarkable", never as "underperforming by 11 points".
  • Low-income counts changed. Sheridan's reported low-income share jumped 9.8 points in one year, which looks like a reporting change. If that figure is inflated, every poverty-adjusted comparison here is slightly too generous to Sheridan, not too harsh.
  • District grades use the high-school formula applied to all students in the district, so a district grade is not an average of its schools' grades.
  • Twins are not causes. Matching on three variables leaves out everything else — teacher supply, funding, mobility, local economy. The twin gap is a question worth asking, not a verdict.
