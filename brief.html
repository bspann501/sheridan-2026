Sheridan School District · September 2026

Four decisions that move Sheridan from a B to an A

The 2026 results are good and the improvement is real. They also point, with unusual precision, at a small number of places where this district is leaving performance on the table — and at four moves it can make with people and practices it already has.

The finding

Sheridan's ceiling is not achievement. It is growth — for the students who start behind, in the elementary grades, in mathematics.

Students arrive testing well and graduate well: achievement sits at the 76th percentile of Arkansas districts and graduation at 94.8%. But three of the eleven indicators that determine the district's letter grade are rated 2 out of 4, and all three measure how much ground students gain in a year. Every one of the four decisions below targets one of those three ratings.

What the grade is waiting on

Eleven ratings. Three of them are the problem.

Arkansas builds the district letter grade from eleven equally weighted indicators, each rated 1 to 4 — not from the total score. Sheridan already rates like an A district on graduation and merit. The gap is concentrated, and it is entirely in growth.

Amber marks a rating of 2. The median A district scores 4 on every achievement and growth indicator and 3 on graduation and merit — which is to say Sheridan's readiness work is already there, and its growth work is not.

Basis

What this rests on, and what it does not

Confidence is high on

  • The diagnosis. The campus split and the three ratings of 2 come straight from the state's own file and are not matters of interpretation.
  • The internal precedents. Every magnitude quoted for a Sheridan campus is the state's published figure for that campus, year over year.
  • The direction of the fix. All four decisions move indicators that are, by formula, a third of the district's grade.

Confidence is lower on

  • Attribution. The state publishes outcomes, not practices. Which specific change produced East End's fourth-grade math gain is an inference your own staff can confirm in a morning — and should, before it is copied.
  • Reported poverty. District low-income share rose 9.8 points in one year, which looks like a reporting change. Peer comparisons here use the reported figure.
  • Small groups. Subgroup movements at a single campus can represent a handful of students and should not drive staffing decisions on their own.
